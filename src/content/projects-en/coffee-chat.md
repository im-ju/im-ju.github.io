## Results

For the first time, what new hires actually went through, from recruiting to their first months, was collected in one place, and one finding turned into a real change.

- **23 people, all one-to-one.** We met each new hire for 30 to 45 minutes over two weeks, 13 to 28 May 2026. I took 9 of them; the rest were split across the team.
- **About 580 comments → a top-10 list of common issues and about 40 action items.** Issues were ranked by how often they came up, and the action items were split into quick wins, medium term and long term.
- **A verbal briefing to the executive team, and one change in practice.** Cross-team requests were moved into a single Slack channel.

## The problem

In April 2026, colleagues raised complaints in a team meeting about the recruiting process and how interviews were handled. Yet nobody had ever collected what the new hires, the people who had just gone through that process, had actually experienced. There were complaints but no evidence, so there was no way to decide what to fix first. The coffee chats started as an action item from that meeting.

## What I did

**I decided what to listen for first.** I split the purpose into three lines (the hiring process, onboarding, working life), wrote a question guide for what to check on each, and set the schedule. How many people each of us would meet was my lead's call.

**I met 9 people myself.** The team split the 23 new hires between us, and I interviewed 9 of them one-to-one.

**I consolidated and analysed about 580 comments on my own.** I brought everyone's notes into one place and sorted them into four areas: recruiting, onboarding, working life, and team and leadership. The top 10 common issues came from how many times the same point was raised. I used AI for the analysis.

**I made the report easy to act on.** I built an HTML report myself with a summary, the top 10 issues and about 40 action items by area. Splitting the actions into quick wins, medium term and long term made it easy to pick what to do first. I used it to brief the executive team verbally.

## Where I got stuck

I had prepared the question list carefully, but some conversations did not go as planned. Everyone talks in their own way and their own order. When I tried to stick to the order of my questions, the conversation stalled.

## What I learned

Even the conversations that left the plan told me something about the person, in directions the question list would never have reached. A question list is where a conversation starts, not a script it has to follow.
